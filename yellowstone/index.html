1. The Big Picture

The Scale: Yellowstone is massive—larger than Rhode Island and Delaware combined. It’s laid out as a "Figure 8" road system. Driving from one side to the other takes hours, and traffic or bison jams can add huge delays.

When to go: June through September are the best months. July and August are peak season (crowded but fully open). Spring and Fall are beautiful but you risk road closures due to snow.

Don't Forget the Tetons: Grand Teton National Park is directly south of Yellowstone. They share a border! If you have the time, you should absolutely spend 1-2 days exploring the Tetons and the town of Jackson Hole.

2. Where to Stay

The "Split Stay"

Because the park is so huge, staying in one hotel the whole time means you will spend 3-5 hours a day just driving back and forth to see the sights. We highly recommend a "Split Stay" strategy.

Leg 1: The West/South (2-3 nights) Stay near Old Faithful, Grant Village, or West Yellowstone. Focus on the famous geothermal features (Grand Prismatic, Old Faithful) and Yellowstone Lake.
Leg 2: The North/East (1-2 nights) Stay near Mammoth Hot Springs or Canyon Village. Focus on the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, wildlife in Hayden/Lamar Valleys, and the Mammoth terraces.

Pro-tip: Park lodges (booked via Xanterra) open 13 months in advance and sell out quickly. Book early, or look for cancellations!
